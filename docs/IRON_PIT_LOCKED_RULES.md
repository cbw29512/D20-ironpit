# Iron Pit locked rules

This is the single index of **Chris-locked** Iron Pit rules. It exists so those locks are not lost in chat.

It does **not** replace `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md`. That file remains the detailed product/combat contract. Architecture, battlefield UI, and pregen construction stay in their existing docs. If this index and a cited file disagree, stop and reconcile in the cited file. Do not write a third copy of the same rule.

## Lycanthrope forms and precombat entry — 2026-10-22 (supersedes damage-optimal selection)

- **2014 lycanthropes always begin combat in their printed beast (animal) form**, regardless of its damage relative to humanoid or hybrid form. Select this as fight-local starting state before initiative at no Action or Bonus Action cost. Never substitute hybrid form as the default.
- **Retain and compile every printed legal form**, including humanoid and hybrid where applicable, with form-specific stats, equipment, attacks, Multiattack sequences, restrictions and Shapechanger options. These remain available to the universal form-change system for legal later transitions; starting in beast form does not delete or prohibit other forms.
- Honor printed form restrictions and costs for any **mid-fight** transformation. Cards remain immutable, the active form is fight-local state, and one source-neutral form/attack resolver must work for every lycanthrope and compatible homebrew creature. Preserve the printed trait in the log/card. Do not mark a monster fully admitted merely because its opening form works.
- This deterministic beast-form entry is an explicit **Iron Pit tactical house rule**, not a RAW requirement to begin transformed; it replaces the prior highest-expected-damage opening selection.

## Canonical combatant form

- **One completed combatant, one engine:** Monster and Character are blank-form authoring modes; both compile into `CombatantDefinition → CombatantTemplate` and fresh fight state. Monster authors enter source-final values; character builders derive values from edition-specific scores, proficiency, weapons, armor and features. Named abilities never select runtime logic. Homebrew uses the same typed primitives as official cards; rejected unsupported mechanics remain blocked. The authoritative details are in [the Rules Contract](IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md) under *Universal combatant form and ability resolution*, and the [universal card contract](UNIVERSAL_CARD_COMBAT_CONTRACT.md).

## Universal gaze initiative and automatic eye aversion — 2026-10-20

**User-approved Iron Pit exception:** For any *source-declared avertible gaze or visible-appearance passive*, compare the gaze source and target using the **existing resolved normal initiative order** (including normal RAW totals and established tie-breaking rules). If the source precedes the target, the target **cannot avert its eyes** and must make the printed saving throw when otherwise eligible. If the target precedes the source, it **automatically averts its eyes** and avoids that initial save. Apply this source-target comparison whenever an eligible passive turn-start gaze decision arises; do not invent an independent roll, extra action or surprise state. Initiative remains the same for the encounter.

Averted eyes are **source-scoped**, not the global Blinded condition. Preserve the printed penalty against that source (for example, attack-roll Disadvantage against the Sea Hag), and printed sight, eligibility, immunity and timing requirements. A genuinely unseen/immune/out-of-range gaze source cannot force a save solely by winning initiative. This replaces surprise-only automatic eye-aversion for the Pit; unrelated non-avertible visual Actions remain governed by their own card. For multiple gaze sources, use normal initiative priority and resolve independently. The source's effect (Frightened, Restrained, Petrified, etc.) and DC/duration/repeat saves are always the standard universal mechanics declared on its immutable card; 2014 and 2024 source values remain separate.

## Universal decision priority — user-locked 2026-10-18

**For ANY game decision in which multiple combatants could act or respond, check combatants in descending initiative order: highest initiative first.** This includes competing reactions, counters, interrupts, and other simultaneous eligible choices. Apply the same initiative priority to monsters and pregens, independent of names or classes. The active combatant still takes its normal initiative turn; this rule settles *competition between eligible decision-makers*, not a grant of extra turns.

**Universal algorithm:** On each decision event, scan initiative order from highest to lowest. For each candidate, check whether it can legally make that decision (trigger, ability, range, perception, required resources, action/reaction availability, applicable conditions). If ineligible, skip without spending anything. The first eligible combatant makes the decision and expends any required resource **only if it takes the action**. If the decision resolves/cancels the event, stop checking that event. If an unresolved event still permits another decision, continue in initiative order. For the next independent event, begin a new scan from the highest initiative, skipping candidates who have exhausted their applicable resources. Use existing initiative tie-breaking and turn/resource refresh rules; do not invent an additional tie policy here.

**Counterspell example:** A dragon casts spell A. Inspect highest-initiative potential reactor first. If that combatant has a reaction (1), can legally Counterspell, and does so, spend its reaction (1 → 0); once spell A is canceled, nobody else spends a reaction countering spell A. Dragon casts spell B: restart from highest initiative; the first wizard is skipped (reaction 0), so the next eligible wizard counters. If nobody can counter, spell B resolves normally. This uses the universal event/reaction scheduler, never a monster-specific branch.

## Engineering

- **Audit and reuse first.** Before any new mechanic, decompose the printed behavior and search the existing hero and monster primitive inventory. Reuse or parameterize a match. Compose from existing primitives when one named ability is several mechanics. Unique code is allowed only for a genuinely unique remainder.
- **Universal conditions.** Prone is Prone, Grappled is Grappled, Frightened is Frightened, Poisoned is Poisoned, and the same for every other shared condition, save, Advantage, and Disadvantage. The card supplies parameters. The engine supplies the mechanic.
- **Printed names in the log.** The player log and card keep the exact source ability name. Engine dispatch uses generic primitive IDs, never class/monster/ability-name branches.
- **2014 families first, then bind 2024.** Finish the 2014 source roster family before expanding an independent 2024 mechanic. After a 2014 primitive is certified, audit 2024 for direct reuse. Edition cards supply printed numbers; do not duplicate engine behavior.
- **Do not stall a family on one blocker.** If a monster, item, spell, or feature hits a real blocker (needs new engine support, RAW ambiguity, missing primitive, or a Chris decision), park that card. Record it below with the printed name, the blocker, and why it cannot ship yet. Keep moving to the next creature or family. Come back to parked items only after the rest of the current family is finished. Do not guess, invent a special case, or strip a printed mechanic to make the card READY. Unsupported outcome-changing mechanics still fail closed.

Authority: `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md` §1.1, §2, §25; `SOUL.md`; `AGENTS.md` semantic-reuse, 2014-first monster order, and uncertainty gate.

- **2014 Immutable Form is arena-neutral only while no supported hostile form-alter effect exists.** The current replacement-form system changes the acting combatant's own form; it does not alter an opposing golem. Keep the printed trait in source/card/log metadata. If a hostile Polymorph/form-alter capability becomes supported, remove this arena-neutral classification and bind the universal form-alter immunity before certifying affected golems.

## Death and dying

- **Universal ability-score reduction / zero-score death (confirmed 2026-10-19):** For **any** of the six ability scores (Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma), a cumulative reduction to **0 immediately kills the target**, including pregens at positive HP. Do **not** route score-zero deaths through HP-zero/death saves; stop later attacks and effects against a Dead target. All live score changes and derived modifier changes exist only in mutable, per-battle `CombatantState`; original monster/pregen card scores are **immutable**. Rebuild fresh state from each original card **after every battle**, restoring all six printed scores regardless of the source's tabletop rest-based recovery text. Within a battle, reductions remain cumulative until removed by an explicitly supported effect (such as Greater Restoration); they do not expire each round. Source printed rest/duration wording remains intact as source metadata. Shadow's 1d4 Strength Drain accompanies its normal necrotic damage as a separate on-hit state rider, not another damage type. Its delayed 1d4-hour corpse spawning is not an in-fight summon under the Pit's no-summons rule. Universal zero-score death is an explicit Iron Pit rule rather than a claim of standard tabletop 5e RAW.
- **One hit, one damage event — 2014 RAW clarification (2026-10-19).** A single Bite dealing piercing plus poison is **one attack roll, one hit, and one HP-damage event**. Roll and resolve each typed component using its own immunity/resistance/vulnerability, then apply their adjusted sum once to Temporary HP/HP and the existing zero-HP/death-save lifecycle. A component that brings a pregen to 0 HP does **not** make another component of that same hit a new hit or an extra failed Death Saving Throw. Subsequent independent hits on a dying pregen use standard damage-at-zero rules (one failed save per normal damaging hit, two for a critical, plus the instant-death threshold). When the whole hit defeats a monster at 0 HP, that monster is no longer a legal target for subsequent attacks or Multiattack slots. Existing source-defined survival/replacement effects take precedence. This corrects the briefly proposed per-component house rule: **do not implement it**.

- **Antimagic Susceptibility is terminal in the Iron Pit.** An applied semantic `antimagic` effect kills a susceptible combatant. Authority: `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md` §13, Antimagic susceptibility.
- **Dispel Magic uses Stunned for susceptible creatures.** Reuse the shared 10-round debuff; this supersedes the earlier Dispel-also-kills choice. Authority: `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md` §13, Antimagic susceptibility.
- **Petrified is terminal in the Iron Pit.** When the Petrified debuff is successfully applied, that combatant is immediately Dead for the rest of the match. This is an explicit Iron Pit arena rule, not a statement of tabletop RAW. The universal condition engine owns this consequence; monster/ability names must not dispatch it.
- **Dead is terminal.** No resurrection, rebirth, revive, later stabilization, regeneration, or Death Saving Throw restores a Dead combatant in that match. The Pit deity restores combatants only after combat.
- **Death Saving Throws only while dying.** They occur only while a character is Unconscious/dying at 0 HP and not Dead. Printed 0-HP replacements intercept the drop to 0 in the same resolution and never leave the combatant Dead first.

Authority: `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md` §13.

## Pit exceptions

These are explicit arena overrides, not RAW changes outside the Pit.

- **No Flyby leave-reach exemption.** An opportunity-attack exemption does not authorize leaving engagement.
- **No teleport relocation, plane shift, ethereal/incorporeal movement state, summoning, or vertical flight.**
- **Natural 1 uses RAW (2026-10-20).** A natural 1 on an attack roll is an automatic miss, not a turn-ending fumble. Unspent attacks/Bonus Actions remain legal. Initiative uses the rolled total plus modifiers; a natural 1 has no special bucket.
- **Teleport only clears matching movement debuffs in place.** Grappled, Restrained, and other `ends_on_teleport` / ground-snare effects may end. Grid x/y does not change.

Authority: `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md` §7, Attack natural 1, §10.1, §20.

Authority: `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md` §20.2.

- **A buff cancels the matching debuff.** Pairing is by condition identity and modifier kind, never by spell/monster/class name. An already-active matching counter-buff suppresses the current condition and causes a new copy of that debuff to fail closed.
- **Debuffs are checked at the start of the creature's turn** before it acts.
- **Calm blocks new fear.** 2014 Calm Emotions uses the shared charm/frighten suppression / condition-immunity primitives. While that suppression is active, a new Frightened copy does not land.
- **Cards are immutable.** Fight mutation belongs only to temporary combat state.
- **Permanent source buffs/debuffs remain defined even when their triggers are absent.** Any printed permanent passive that grants a buff, imposes a weakness, or reacts to an environment is retained on the immutable source card and exposed as a stable BUFFS/DEBUFFS entry. Its current **active state** is a typed, source-neutral predicate (sunlight, water exposure, target condition, ally proximity, etc.), never permission to add/delete the trait from the card or to start an invented duration. Show **grey/inactive** when the predicate is false and **bright white/active** when it is true. When future supported sources create the context, the same declaration activates without special-casing a monster. On battle reset the original permanent trait remains; only fight-local effects/exposures reset. A context-absent passive may be inert **now**, but it may only be marked fully implemented when the declared effect is implemented for a legal future trigger; otherwise preserve the exact unsupported obligation fail-closed.
- **Permanent exposure examples (2014):** Shadow's `Sunlight Weakness` is Disadvantage on **attack rolls, all ability checks, and saving throws** while in genuine sunlight (not ordinary bright light). Fire Elemental's `Water Susceptibility` is exactly **1 Cold damage per 5 feet moved through water or per gallon splashed**, subject to ordinary typed defenses; it is **not** Cold vulnerability or a water damage type. No ambient sunlight/water is invented. Permanent passive schema and UI must be reusable for future environmental content and source-legal homebrew.
- **All printed monster limited-use resources are fight-scoped.** A printed `1/Day`, `3/Day`, or `recharges after a short/long rest` label sets its allowed uses **within one Iron Pit fight**; it never creates real-world day/rest tracking between battles. On each new fight, construct fresh combat state from the immutable monster template with its complete printed resource allowance. Do not replenish a spent limited-use resource during the same fight unless an independently supported combat recharge trigger explicitly does so. This applies to every monster, not just Dretch; keep original printed source/card wording unchanged.
- **Match end resets all effects.**
- **Printed 24-hour immunities are match-scoped.** They last for the rest of the current fight only. Do not simulate calendar 24 hours and do not persist immunity across fights.

Authority: `docs/IRON_PIT_RULES_CONTRACT.md` §3, §8 Turn-start buff versus debuff, Calm Emotions, §10.1, §16.

## Casters and pregen weapons

- **Druid role-based Wild Shape AI — 2026-10-09.** Existing **caster** Druids (both editions) prioritize spellcasting/healing rather than automatically Wild Shaping. They consider Wild Shape only when severely hurt; initial adjustable Iron Pit heuristic is **original HP ≤ one-third maximum**, and the current tactical score compares **net defensive HP** with a legal same-action-cost self-heal. Ally healing and condition removal retain their priority; multi-target and uncertain-value healing are not suppressed. The next AI stage compares a legal offensive spell's expected damage with the effective form buffer at HP below the emergency threshold. At **HP ≤ half the emergency threshold** (initially one-sixth max HP), immediate form survival has priority; at higher emergency HP, a spell wins when its expected damage equals or exceeds the available form buffer and transformation would consume the same action or remove that spell option. Source-declared retained spells are not counted as lost. This comparison is an **Arena AI heuristic, never RAW damage or HP rules**. Incoming danger now uses the highest plausible **single enemy printed weapon/save Action** including Multiattack, reach and one normal move. When that source-grounded pressure could exhaust current HP plus existing Temporary HP, survival form priority overrides the normal spell damage score. This is **conservative lookahead, not a precise hit chance, movement path, enemy focus-fire prediction or RAW damage roll**. Incoming spell damage is now included by previewing the next-turn Action budget without altering combat state, reusing existing legal spell, slot, targeting, saving throw, attack-roll and resistance scoring; for multi-target spells only the Druid's expected damage counts. This is expected damage, not the probability of a maximum-dice kill, and it excludes future spell repositioning, protective-spell values and multi-enemy sequencing.
